Emi A. Lee

Machine learning researcher, UCSD Design Lab · Data Engineer, Fidelity Investments

I work on human–AI interaction: how people come to trust and make decisions with intelligent systems, and how machine learning can model the imperfect, human-generated signals — facial expressions, biometrics, behavior — that shape those interactions. My interests span behavioral modeling, applied ML in cognitive and social domains, and predictive modeling with messy real-world data.

I am a research assistant in the Human eXtended Intelligence group at the UCSD Design Lab (PI Nadir Weibel), where I build ML pipelines that predict social signals in doctor–patient interactions. By day I am a data engineer at Fidelity Investments.

Research

Social Signal Prediction

UCSD Design Lab · HXI · 2025 – present

End-to-end ML pipeline predicting social signals — warmth, empathy, dominance — from facial Action Unit time-series in 108 doctor–patient clinical interactions, with a three-tier feature architecture (255 AU features) evaluated under leave-one-group-out cross-validation.

Trust in Autonomous Vehicles

UCSD Design Lab · HXI · 2023 – 2024

How psychosocial traits and risk-benefit attitudes shape trust in autonomously powered vehicles, studied through VR/XR. Built the ML framework for evaluation, testing, and ablation across 7 models and 1,450+ participants; published at CHI '25.

Affective Gaming

UCSD Design Lab · 2023

Affect representations in multiplayer FPS games and their impact on player experience, strategy, and stress — with ML models for biosensing and valence detection from heart rate, blood pressure, and galvanic skin response.

ML Emulation of Energy Models

Agile Electrification · 2023 – 2024

Machine learning surrogates that emulate slow physics-based simulation for energy consumption prediction, plus geocoding and location-verification tooling; culminated in an industry-facing whitepaper.

Zenetic Inter-Cultural Computing

Kyoto University · Prof. Naoko Tosa · 2023 – 2024

Remastered prototype of the Zenetic Computing project: generative neural models that interactively build narratives on Japanese art forms in the Koan literary tradition, demoed for Smithsonian curators.

Clinical Attrition NLP

Scripps Research · NIH All of Us · 2022

NLP and sentiment-analysis pipelines identifying factors behind clinical subject attrition in longevity science and precision medicine, presented to the Scripps data science team.
